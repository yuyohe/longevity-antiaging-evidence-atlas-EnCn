## Main Results / 主要结果

EN: Of 3080 studies, 47 studies involving 11,356 surgeries and 13 CPET variables were included. Meta-analysis and GRADE assessment showed lower peak oxygen uptake (peak V˙O₂) increased the risk of perioperative complications by 2.04-3.02 times (P < 0.001), using a cutoff point of 15 mL/kg/min or peak V˙O2 expressed as %pred. A higher minute ventilation to carbon dioxide production (V˙E/V˙CO₂) slope was associated with a 2.44-fold increased risk (95% CI [1.82-3.27], P < 0.001), with a cutoff point of 35 (high-level). Limited studies suggest peak V˙O₂ (L/min) and V˙E/V˙CO₂ slope relate to cancer-specific and all-cause mortality. Evidence for variables such as V˙O₂ at anaerobic threshold and peak ventilation remains low.
