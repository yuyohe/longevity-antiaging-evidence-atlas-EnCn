## Main Results / 主要结果

EN: The sample comprised 4536 centenarians (mean age 102.8 years, SD 2.6); 80.9% were women, 63.9% lived in rural areas, and the mean follow-up was 28.7 months (SD 22.9), during which period 88.9% of the participants died. Participants were classified as having unfavorable (17.9%, n = 813), intermediate (70.5%, n = 3200), or favorable (11.5%, n = 523) lifestyles. Each 1-point increase in the lifestyle score was associated with a 6.9% longer survival time (TR = 1.069, 95% CI 1.049-1.089). Compared with centenarians with unfavorable lifestyles, those with intermediate and favorable lifestyles lived an additional 3.79 months (95% CI 1.57-6.19) and 11.04 months (95% CI 7.10-15.43), respectively. Results were robust across sensitivity analyses.
