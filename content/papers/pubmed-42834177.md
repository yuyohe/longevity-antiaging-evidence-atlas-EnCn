## Main Results / 主要结果

EN: Autophagy dysfunction and senescence are established drivers of aging, but their potential interaction remains poorly understood. Here we show that age-related decline of chaperone-mediated autophagy (CMA), a pathway for selective lysosomal protein degradation, changes senescent cell properties and impairs their immune clearance. CMA-deficient cells undergo senescence but acquire proteomic, metabolic and secretory features resembling those in aged senescent cells. Their senescence-associated secretory phenotype exhibits enhanced pro-senescence effects on neighboring cells and inhibits macrophage CMA, which impairs their ability to engulf senescent cells.
