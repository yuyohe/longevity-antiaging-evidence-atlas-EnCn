## Main Results / 主要结果

EN: Salt-sensitive hypertension (SSH) is a complex and heterogeneous phenotype characterized by an abnormal blood pressure response to dietary salt intake. While genetic factors have been extensively explored, emerging evidence highlights the pivotal role of epigenetic mechanisms (DNA methylation, histone modifications and non-coding RNAs) in modulating gene expression without altering the DNA sequence. These modifications respond dynamically to environmental stimuli such as diet, aging, stress and prenatal conditions, contributing to both the development and progression of SSH. This review summarizes current knowledge on the epigenetic regulation of genes involved in sodium handling, vascular tone and inflammation, focusing on pathways such as the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, the Klotho-Wnt5a-RhoA axis and the influence of the intrauterine environment.
