## Main Results / 主要结果

EN: The cohort comprised 4782 participants (mean age: 60 years, 40% women) with a median eGFR of 46 ml/min per 1.73 m2, and autoimmune kidney disease in 22%. Higher agalactosylated IgG was associated with lower eGFR (-4%/SD; 97.9% confidence interval [CI]: -6% to -3%) and higher mortality (hazard ratio [HR]: 1.39; 97.9% CI: 1.19-1.62). Conversely, higher digalactosylated and sialylated IgG were associated with higher eGFR (+5%) and lower mortality (HR: 0.68 and 0.80, respectively). Associations with urinary albumin-to-creatine ratio [UACR] and KF varied by age, sex, and autoimmune status. Core fucosylation and bisecting N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) showed weaker associations, primarily with UACR. MR suggested bidirectional causal relationships between eGFR and specific N-glycan traits.
