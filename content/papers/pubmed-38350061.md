## Main Results / 主要结果

EN: Among 502,383 participants (mean [SD] age, 56.5 [8.1] years; 54.4% female), there were 7,668 cases of all-cause dementia and 10,334 strokes. In longitudinal analyses, after controlling for cardiovascular risk factors, participants with insomnia, daytime napping, and dozing were associated with increased risk of any stroke (HR 1.05, 95% CI 1.01-1.11, p = 8.53 × 10-3; HR 1.09, 95% CI 1.05-1.14, p = 3.20 × 10-5; HR 1.19, 95% CI 1.08-1.32, p = 4.89 × 10-4, respectively). Almost all sleep measures were associated with dementia risk (all p < 0.001, except insomnia). Cross-sectional analyses identified associations between napping, snoring, and MRI markers of SVD (all p < 0.001). MR analyses supported a causal link between genetically predicted insomnia and increased stroke risk (OR 1.31, 95% CI 1.13-1.51, p = 0.00072), but not with dementia or SVD markers.
