## Main Results / 主要结果

EN: ENDS use induced acute changes: HR increased by 7.2 bpm (during intervention), SBP by 3.4 mmHg, DBP by 3.3 mmHg, and MBP by 3.7 mmHg. SV decreased, and HRV analysis indicated a shift towards sympathetic dominance. Conventional cigarettes caused similar, but more pronounced increases in SBP, DBP, and MBP during intervention (e.g., SBP 2.9 mmHg higher than ENDS). ENNDS resulted in smaller increases in HR (5.6 bpm less than ENDS), SBP (2.7 mmHg less), DBP (2.1 mmHg less), and MBP (2.6 mmHg less) compared to ENDS.

## Conclusion Boundary / 结论边界

EN: ENDS and conventional cigarettes both induce acute cardiovascular stress, characterized by increased HR and BP, reduced SV, and sympathetic activation. The hemodynamic and autonomic effects of ENDS closely resemble those of conventional cigarettes. Although attenuated, ENNDS use was not physiologically neutral, raising concerns regarding its cardiovascular safety as an alternative to traditional smoking.
