## Main Results / 主要结果

EN: Self-reported current exercise habits were significantly associated with lower all-cause mortality risk after multivariate adjustment (fully adjusted HR = 0.835, 95% CI: 0.773-0.902, p < 0.001). SO2 emissions showed a weak association with survival risk in univariate analysis (HR = 1.023, 95% CI: 0.942-1.111, p = 0.585). The association estimate was further attenuated after multivariate adjustment (fully adjusted HR = 1.001, 95% CI: 0.874-1.146, p = 0.992). Soot-related indicators showed no significant mortality associations. The interaction between exercise habits and each of the four industrial air pollution indicators was not significant (all p > 0.05).

## Conclusion Boundary / 结论边界

EN: Self-reported current exercise habits were consistently associated with lower all-cause mortality among Chinese older adults, and this association was robust to adjustment for covariates. No statistically significant multiplicative interaction was detected between exercise habits and any of the four pollution indicators. Continued attention to industrial emission control remains warranted, while promoting habitual exercise among older adults may be beneficial in areas with industrial emissions.
