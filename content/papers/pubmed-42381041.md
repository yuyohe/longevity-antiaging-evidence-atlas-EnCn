## Main Results / 主要结果

EN: During the mean 28-year follow-up period, 20.5% of the participants died. Long hours were directly associated with decreased risk of all-cause mortality, but only at the between-pair level; there were no associations with biological aging. However, long hours were indirectly associated with an increased risk of all-cause and CVD mortality through a higher BMI and smoking, and with an increased risk of CVD mortality through lower LTPA levels. Long hours were also indirectly associated with accelerated biological aging through smoking.
