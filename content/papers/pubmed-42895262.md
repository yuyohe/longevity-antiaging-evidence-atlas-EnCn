## Main Results / 主要结果

EN: Over median follow-up periods of 4, 6, and 8 years, 244, 2,693, and 1,986 participants in the ELSA, the SHARE, and the HRS cohorts developed osteoporosis, respectively. Higher baseline FI was independently associated with greater osteoporosis risk across all cohorts, with adjusted hazard ratios of 1.43 (95% confidence interval (CI) = 1.36-1.50, P < 0.001) in the HRS, 1.36 (95% CI = 1.19-1.57, P < 0.001) in the ELSA, and 1.39 (95% CI = 1.33-1.45, P < 0.001) in the SHARE cohort. We identified four FI trajectories. Compared with the low frailty group, participants in higher frailty trajectory groups generally exhibited increased risks of osteoporosis across cohorts. Nonlinear analyses pointed to threshold effects in the HRS and the SHARE, with steeper risk increases at lower FI levels. Grip strength partially mediated this association.
